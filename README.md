# Superstore-Analysis-DIY-Project-3-

# Problem Statement of Superstore Analysis

Retail businesses generate vast amounts of transactional data across products, regions, and customer segments, yet many struggle to translate this raw data into actionable business insight. A superstore chain operating across multiple U.S. regions sells products across three broad categories — Furniture, Office Supplies, and Technology — to Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office customer segments, using varying shipping modes (Standard Class, Second Class, First Class, Same Day).
While the company records detailed order-level data including sales, quantity, discount, and profit, it currently lacks a clear understanding of which factors are driving profitability and which are eroding it. Preliminary patterns suggest that certain sub-categories (like Tables and Bookcases) and high-discount transactions are associated with significant losses, even when sales figures appear strong. Without a systematic analysis, the company cannot answer key questions such as:
Which products and regions are most profitable, and which are underperforming?
How does discounting affect profit margins, and at what point does it become unsustainable?
Which customer segments and shipping modes contribute most to revenue and profit?
Are there regional or state-level disparities in performance that need strategic attention?
This lack of visibility limits the company's ability to make informed decisions on pricing, discounting policy, inventory focus, and regional resource allocation.


# Project Description of this Project

This project involves conducting a comprehensive Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) on the Superstore dataset to uncover sales and profitability trends across products, regions, customer segments, and shipping modes. 

This Dataset Includes 
Ship Mode : The method used to deliver the order, such as Standard Class, Second Class, First Class, or Same Day.
Segment :The type of customer, such as Consumer, Corporate, or Home Office.
Country: The country where the customer or order is located.
City: The city where the order was placed or delivered.
State: The state where the customer or order is located.
Postal Code: The postal or ZIP code of the customer's location.
Region: The geographical region, such as Central, East, South, or West.
Category: The main product category, such as Furniture, Office Supplies, or Technology.
Sub-Category: A more specific product group within a category, such as Chairs, Phones, Binders, or Storage.
Sales: The total sales amount generated from the order.
Quantity :The number of products or items sold in the order
Discount: The discount percentage or rate given on the product or order.
Profit: The profit or loss earned from the order after considering sales and related costs. A positive value indicates profit, while a negative value indicates loss

# What is Data Wrangling ?

Data wrangling, a crucial phase in the data analysis process, involves preparing and cleaning raw data to make it suitable for analysis. This involves various tasks such as handling missing values, removing duplicates, and transforming data into a consistent format. Effective data wrangling ensures that the data is reliable and ready for exploration, enabling meaningful insights to be derived from it.


# What is the Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)is a vital phase in data analysis involving the examination and visualization of data to uncover patterns ,trends, and insights.in the context of an Airbnb data analysis project, EDA allows researchers to gain a Comprehensive understanding of the dataset's characteristics, distribution and relationships between variables. Through techniques such as summary stastics, data visualization ,and correlation analysis, EDA sets the stage for further investigation and hypothesis testing, uiding subsequent analytical decisions.

# Future Scope

1) Predictive Analytics Integration: Go beyond a purely descriptive EDA. Build predictive models (regression, time series forecasting etc.) to predict future sales, demand and profit trends at a category, regional or seasonal level.
2) Customer Segmentation & Behaviour Analysis: Utilize clustering techniques (RFM analysis, K-Means etc.) on the customer data to group them by purchasing behaviour, allowing for a more refined and targeted marketing strategy.
3) Discount Optimization Model: Create a data-driven discount optimization or pricing model. This model could predict the optimal discount for each sub-category/region to maximise profit while staying competitive.
4) Inventory & Demand Planning :Integrate the analysis with current inventory data to find areas with overstocked/understocked products which should lead to better demand forecasting and improved supply chain management.
5) Real-Time Dashboard Automation :Transform this static analysis into a live automated dashboard (e.g. Power BI/Tableau pointing to a live database) that updates in real time as new sales come in.
6) Geospatial/Market Expansion Analysis: Use the location data to map sales and identify potential areas of business expansion. This could be at a state, city or even neighbourhood level.
7) Integration with External Data: Integrate this dataset with external data sources such as seasonality, economic indicators, competitor pricing etc. In order to capture additional insights into the drivers of sales and profit.
8) Customer Feedback & Sentiment Analysis: If customer review/feedback data is available, it can be analysed for sentiment to see if and how it is correlating with repeat business or profitability.

# Conclusion

Overall, the sales throughout all the product categories as well as different regions look normal or strong. The profit however is highly distorted because too much discount was given throughout the company, especially in Tables and Bookcases sub-category where most of the sale recorded in those sub-categories given very large profit which means 50% and above discount is usually results in loss despite very strong sale. The variable performance among different region, customer type and the ship mode also suggest that business model has to take regional difference into account and a customized strategic decision, based on the data is the best approach to achieve higher profit. Based on the findings, the data used in this project has provided good evidence for business on deciding their strategies, by setting more reasonable discount strategy, emphasizing the right product at the right region with efficient strategy, improving customer engagement and analyzing those failing segments are all effective ways for the business to improve their overall profit, for retail, having insight data to generate strategies is a valuable factor to succeed.
