# Superstore-Analysis-DIY-Project-3-
Retail businesses generate vast amounts of transactional data across products, regions, and customer segments, yet many struggle to translate this raw data into actionable business insight. A superstore chain operating across multiple U.S. regions sells products across three broad categories — Furniture, Office Supplies, and Technology — to Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office customer segments, using varying shipping modes (Standard Class, Second Class, First Class, Same Day).
While the company records detailed order-level data including sales, quantity, discount, and profit, it currently lacks a clear understanding of which factors are driving profitability and which are eroding it. Preliminary patterns suggest that certain sub-categories (like Tables and Bookcases) and high-discount transactions are associated with significant losses, even when sales figures appear strong. Without a systematic analysis, the company cannot answer key questions such as:
Which products and regions are most profitable, and which are underperforming?
How does discounting affect profit margins, and at what point does it become unsustainable?
Which customer segments and shipping modes contribute most to revenue and profit?
Are there regional or state-level disparities in performance that need strategic attention?
This lack of visibility limits the company's ability to make informed decisions on pricing, discounting policy, inventory focus, and regional resource allocation.
